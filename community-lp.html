ABOUT THE PROGRAM

What Is Community Liquidity?

Liquidity providers (LPs) deposit supported token pairs into a decentralized exchange pool. These assets help other users swap between tokens. In return, LPs may receive a share of eligible trading fees according to the pool rules and their position. Fees are not guaranteed and depend on actual trading activity and pool conditions.

HOW TO PARTICIPATE

Before You Become an LP

Take time to understand the pool and the risks. The steps below are an overview, not a guarantee that a particular pool or position is suitable for you.

Review the Liquidity Center

Check the current pool details, token pair, network, fee tier, and available on-chain information.

Open the Correct Uniswap Pool

Use the official link provided through the Liquidity Center. Confirm that you are on Polygon and that the token addresses match the intended pair.

Connect Your Wallet

Connect a compatible wallet, such as MetaMask. Keep enough POL for Polygon network transaction fees.

Understand the Position Settings

Review the amount of each asset, price range, fee tier, and any transaction costs before confirming. Concentrated liquidity may require active monitoring.

Monitor Your Position

Check whether your position remains in range, review earned fees if available, and understand the conditions for withdrawing liquidity.

IMPORTANT INFORMATION

Understand the Risks

Liquidity provision involves risk

Providing liquidity is not the same as holding tokens in a wallet. The value and composition of your position can change as market prices move.

  • Impermanent loss: changes in the relative prices of the deposited tokens can leave an LP with a different value than simply holding them.
  • Price-range risk: in concentrated liquidity pools, a position can move out of its selected range and become one-sided, depending on the pool design.
  • Market risk: JJS and paired-token prices can fluctuate. You may lose some or all of the value you deposit.
  • Smart contract and network risk: decentralized exchange contracts, wallets, and blockchain networks may involve technical risks.
  • Fees are not guaranteed: trading fees depend on pool activity, position status, and the exchange's fee rules.

Only consider providing assets you can afford to risk. Do your own research and verify all transaction details before signing.
